Smart vertical gardening

CUBIC

Grow your own food. Wherever you are.

A smarter way to bring agriculture into your home. CUBIC is a vertical garden concept that combines automated irrigation, artificial intelligence, and rainwater collection to make growing fruits and vegetables at home simpler and more accessible.

It was created for people who want to grow their own food, improve their eating habits, and have more control over what they consume, even when they have limited space or resources.

The problem

Healthy food should be easier to access.

Access

Organic food is hard to find.

Fruits and vegetables are an essential part of a healthy diet, but in countries such as Colombia, organic products can be harder to find and more expensive than conventional ones.

Origin

It is hard to know how food was grown.

Conventional agricultural products may contain pesticide residues, which makes it difficult for consumers to know exactly how their food was produced.

Space

Farming needs room most people do not have.

Agriculture is usually tied to rural areas, large gardens and open land. Many people in cities or apartments lack the space and resources to grow their own food.

The solution

Bringing agriculture from the countryside into people's homes.

Instead of managing separate systems, CUBIC integrates plant cultivation, automated irrigation, water management and artificial intelligence in one solution. The goal is to make home agriculture easier to understand, assemble, use and manage.

Plants

Plants come separately. Planting takes one step.

Future development

CUBIC does not include plants. You order them on the CUBIC website, either as baby plants that have already started to grow, or as seed pods.

Baby plants

Young plants that are already growing. Place one in a cup and it is planted.

Seed pods

The seed arrives already in soil, inside a paper wrapper. The wrapper is colored by plant: red for strawberry, blue for blueberry, purple for lavender. You do not unwrap it. The pod fits the cup exactly.

Water does the rest

When you add water, the wrapper slowly dissolves. As it breaks down it gives the young plant its first minerals, and it takes the guesswork out of starting a plant.

Plants, colors and the wrapper material are examples of the concept. Ordering will open on the CUBIC website once the product is available.

AI plant identification

Color first. Leaves later.

  1. Future development

    Every pod has a color.

    Red is strawberry, blue is blueberry, purple is lavender. The color is on the paper that wraps each pod, so CUBIC can tell plants apart even when there is only soil.

  2. Future development

    The camera scans the shelf.

    The camera on the column looks at each cup in turn and reads the main color inside the frame.

  3. Future development

    Color becomes a plant.

    Each color is matched to a plant. You do not type anything or pick from a list.

  4. Future development

    Your phone gets the plan.

    The app schedules care for each plant: water, minerals, a mineral ampoule in the water. Reminders arrive on the right day. Dates here are examples.

  5. Future development

    Later, the leaves confirm it.

    Once leaves appear, AI vision can confirm the plant by its shape. Early on, color does the job.

Technology

Accessible parts, working together.

CUBIC combines low-cost electronic components with software and artificial intelligence. The first prototype was built at home with affordable materials.

Simplified diagram of the irrigation logic explored in the prototype.

The initial prototype

  • Arduino Uno
  • Water-level sensor
  • Relay module
  • Water pump
  • Electrical wiring
  • PVC tubing
  • A larger PVC pipe as the internal plant support
  • Four rigid plastic cups as plant containers

To explain the irrigation process during the project presentation, AI-generated demonstration code showed how a relay can switch a pump on at set intervals. The program was then modified with help from the creator's father.

Next steps include a dedicated electronic chip for tighter hardware integration, and a more efficient, better organized irrigation program.

The irrigation demo code is public, so anyone who wants to look under the hood can read it.

A connection to Colombian agriculture

Technology that can create opportunities for local farmers.

If CUBIC grows into a commercial product, its seeds and other agricultural supplies could come from Colombian producers. That would give local farmers a place in a technology-based agricultural initiative and connect traditional agricultural knowledge with new ways of growing food.

The intention is not only to bring agriculture into people's homes, but to keep a link with the people and communities who produce our food.

Potential future collaborations

  • Colombian seed producers
  • Local farmers and agricultural communities
  • Agricultural suppliers
  • Technology and engineering specialists
  • Organizations focused on urban agriculture and sustainable food production

These are future opportunities, not established partnerships.

Behind the project

Started in a school technology course.

Inspiration

Rooted in rural Colombia.

CUBIC comes from a personal connection to agriculture and an interest in making technology useful in everyday life. Growing up in a rural area of Colombia, its creator came to value plants, farming and the relationship between people and the food they eat.

The project also borrows the minimalist philosophy associated with Apple products: technology should feel intuitive, integrated and easy to use, from assembling the system to managing plants in the app.

Development

More than a class assignment.

CUBIC began as an independent idea. Instead of completing a simple classroom task, its creator used the time to build something with potential beyond the classroom.

The first prototype was built at home with the support of her father, an engineer, using affordable materials to test the structure and the irrigation system. It was not completed in every planned respect, and it gave the project its foundation.

The vision

Making agriculture part of everyday life.

CUBIC imagines a future where growing food at home does not take extensive agricultural knowledge, a large space or complicated equipment. By bringing together agriculture, engineering and artificial intelligence, it aims to change how people relate to the food they eat, and to bring technology and traditional agricultural communities closer together.
